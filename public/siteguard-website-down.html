FREE SMALL-BUSINESS WEBSITE GUIDE

Website down? A calm, practical response checklist.

A customer may be trying to book, enquire or pay while your site is unavailable. Use this checklist to distinguish a real outage from a local connection problem and get the right people involved.

Independent checks can help; no single monitor can guarantee availability or prove that every form or checkout works.

First 10 minutes: establish what failed

  1. Check the exact page. Note the URL, approximate time, and any HTTP error or browser message. Try the homepage and the specific booking/contact page separately.
  2. Confirm from another connection. Try a mobile connection or another trusted network. A single failed check may reflect local connectivity, an ISP or a DNS resolver.
  3. Identify the boundary. Can your domain resolve? Does HTTPS work? Does the server respond with an error such as 500 or 503? Are only third-party booking or form services failing?
  4. Avoid panicked changes. Record the error and recent changes before restarting services, changing DNS or clearing caches. Preserve diagnostic information.

Find the responsible system

Domain and DNS

Check domain expiry, authoritative DNS records and whether recent DNS changes have finished propagating. Confirm the intended server address.

Hosting

Review provider status announcements, resource limits, deployment logs and database availability. An HTTP 200 response does not guarantee forms work.

HTTPS / SSL

Inspect certificate expiry and the browser's certificate error. A broken renewal can stop visitors even if the web server is running.

When customers need the website now

If enquiries, bookings or orders are interrupted, provide an alternative contact method on a channel you already control, such as your business profile or a verified social account. Give a factual status update; avoid promising a fix time you cannot substantiate.

Where suitable, advise customers not to resubmit payments repeatedly until you can confirm whether an earlier transaction succeeded. Don't publish private incident logs or customer data.

Recovery verification

  • Check the page from another connection after the host reports recovery.
  • Test one genuine enquiry or booking flow under your normal safe testing process; a simple availability monitor cannot do this for you.
  • Confirm SSL, important DNS records, and customer-facing pages still work.
  • Log the incident and identify whether alerts arrived promptly enough to be useful.

Prevent the next silent outage

For a basic small-business setup, monitor your homepage, contact page and the key booking or enquiry page you own or are authorised to check. Add incident and recovery emails, and review your certificate expiry. Verify the important user journey separately.
