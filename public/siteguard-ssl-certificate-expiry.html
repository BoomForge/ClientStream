FREE SMALL-BUSINESS WEBSITE GUIDE

SSL certificate expiring? What to check before visitors see warnings.

HTTPS certificate issues can block customers even while the server is online. This plain-language checklist helps small-business owners understand expiry, renewal and what website monitoring can and cannot tell them.

Independent checks can help; no single monitor can guarantee availability or prove that every form or checkout works.

Understand the warning

A valid TLS certificate helps browsers establish an encrypted connection and verify the website name. Common certificate warnings can come from an expired certificate, a wrong hostname, an incomplete chain, a device with an incorrect clock or an interception problem. A warning is not by itself proof the whole site is offline.

  1. Check the domain shown. Look closely at www and non-www hostnames and any booking, store or other subdomains. Each must be correctly covered by the certificate in use.
  2. Inspect the expiry date. Review the certificate information from a trusted browser or your hosting control panel and note the issuer and end date.
  3. Check automatic renewal. Confirm domain verification, DNS records and any validation challenge still work. An automatic renewal setting does not guarantee every renewal succeeded.
  4. Check deployment. Some hosts have renewed the certificate but are still serving an old one. Check the certificate presented to actual visitors.

Common failure points

Changed DNS

The renewal service may be checking a different address after a migration. Verify domain ownership and validation records.

Multiple hostnames

The main site may be protected while a booking subdomain or redirect target uses a different certificate.

Renewal without reload

A valid replacement may exist on disk or at the provider, but the running service still presents an older certificate.

What to do before and after a fix

Check the status of certificate renewal with your host, DNS service or technical administrator. Do not bypass HTTPS warnings to enter passwords, card data or customer information. After renewing, verify the correct domain names and certificate dates from a normal customer browser and test important pages.

If you use a CDN or reverse proxy, there may be separate certificates between visitors and the CDN, and between the CDN and your origin. A successful public HTTPS check does not automatically prove the origin's certificate setup is healthy.

A simple monitoring routine

  • Know which business domains and subdomains are commercially important.
  • Confirm the responsible provider and who receives renewal notices.
  • Periodically review HTTPS expiry visibility and incident messages.
  • Verify actual login, booking, form or purchase flows separately from HTTP availability.
